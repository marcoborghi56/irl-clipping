# Clipping playbook: raw IRL vlog footage to short reels

Source: 88 Instagram reels from Marlon, Lacy, Stable Ronaldo (SR) and TJR: per creator, 16 outliers (views far above neighbouring posts) and 6 flops (about half). Eight reviewers wrote per-reel notes; measurements come from `aggregate.csv`. Format tags in the CSV are often wrong (reviewers re-labelled many "irl" reels as stream clips, reposts or edits), so counts below use the reviewers' reading. About 25 outliers are real handheld IRL footage; the rest are stream clips, skits, montages or ads. Weight the IRL ones.

## 1. What makes a moment clip-worthy

Every outlier, in every format, contains one event a stranger can follow with no prior knowledge of the people: something happens, someone reacts. Counts are outliers whose main draw is that type (one label per reel, my synthesis of the notes).

1. **Social collision with a stranger or the public** (9 outliers, nearly all IRL). An ask, offer, compliment, challenge or plea, then the other person's unexpected answer. Examples: Marlon says "What a beautiful dog", then "What a beautiful man" to its flustered owner; SR asks strangers for a fry ("No. No. No."); TJR's $100 burger bounty ends in a payment dispute; Lacy argues with stadium security.
2. **Physical comedy or prank among friends** (6). A friend locked in a "soundproof" sauna screaming; a friend lifted and carried across Shibuya crossing; the handle broken off a classroom door; a friend speaking Spanish to a puppy that ignores him.
3. **Reveal of an outcome, number or object** (10, of which 4 are IRL). The gas price climbing on a Rolls-Royce pump, a car key that turns out to belong to a Lamborghini, a phone dropped off a balcony that splits in two, a new gadget unboxed. Works when the number or object is shown and changes on screen.
4. **Mood piece on trending audio** (5). A deadpan selfie with a song line playing (fireworks behind, a couple kissing, a friend group). These rely on the sound added at posting.
5. **Kid or emotional moment** (4). A niece saying rude words to Spider-Man; a famous player consoling a crying 15-year-old; a friend crying. Sincere emotion, clearly visible.
6. **Celebrity crossover or fan-crowd spectacle** (about 9). Zlatan, LeBron, Drake, crowds mobbing Marlon. Does not transfer (section 8).
7. **Skits, montages, AI animation, ads** (about 15). Not applicable to raw footage.

**Disqualifiers seen in the 24 flops** (one primary reason each):
- Nothing happens on camera: an announcement, promo, pep talk, opinion or advice ("Lacy will be live tonight in Japan", "Go out and win", how much money to start trading). 9 of 24, including 2 sponsor reads.
- In-joke that needs context: a name or bit the viewer does not know ("Shoutout Silky and Adapt", "Aura.", "Bepsy is back...", "I can't do Nathan"). 6 of 24.
- Diffuse summary or static stretch: a "perfect day" recap, 14 s of sunset, 110 s of song reactions. 3 of 24.
- Someone else's content with a tiny personal reaction (an NFL broadcast, a game cutscene). 2 of 24.
- Wrong payoff timing: the peak at 20% then 40 s of talk (Lacy flop02); the result buried past 75% of a 66-74 s clip (TJR flop02, flop06). Also counted here: a parody whose caption gives away the only joke, and a product hand-off with no joke. 4 of 24.

## 2. Hook

- The clip opens mid-action or mid-line. The first spoken word is at 0.0 s in most outliers. Reviewers count "no greeting, already happening" in 8 of 8 (Lacy B), 8 of 8 (TJR B), 7 of 8 (Marlon B) and 5 of 8 (SR B).
- The premise is clear within about 1 s, from the first line ("This is how much it costs to fill up my vintage Rolls-Royce", "Can I get a fry over?", "Yo, the first person to get me a double double...") or from the title caption.
- Flop openings: greetings and context ("Hey there. I don't know if you guys saw...", "Welcome to my caretaker day", "Guys, I'm here at the Giants Cowboys game"), a buzzword list, or a wide quiet shot. (One outlier opens "welcome, I'm Spider-Man" but the event starts within 5 s.)

## 3. Structure, length and payoff position

- **One beat per clip.** Setup, turn, reaction, cut. Long outliers (55-272 s) all keep escalating, with a new beat every few seconds: the gas price ticking up, a new security guard every few minutes, crowd density rising. Long flops have one beat and filler.
- **Length does not separate the groups.** IRL outlier median 24 s (quartiles 17-40 s, 10th-90th percentile 10-57 s); IRL flop median 22 s. Choose length to fit the moment, mostly 15-35 s.
- **Payoff position.** In single-beat outliers the turn lands between about 30% and 90% of runtime; most reviewers put it in the middle to last third (for example Marlon B 5 of 8 at 65-95%, Lacy B 5 of 8 in the last third, SR A 6 of 8 by 30-60%). An early payoff works only if the rest is reaction (Marlon's "beautiful man" turn at 13%, then 19 s of the stranger laughing).
- **Outliers talk less.** 1.3 words/s and speech in 45% of runtime, against 2.5 words/s and 63% for flops. The gaps hold action and reactions. A dense explanation is a warning sign.

## 4. Ending

- Hard cut right after the reaction or the closing line: on a laugh, a face, an apology ("Sorry about that"), a deadpan shot. No outros, no "follow for more", no fade in nearly every outlier.
- Reaction shots after the payoff appear in 4 of 8 Marlon A outliers and none of his flops.

## 5. Caption and title practice

Text is on screen from frame 0 for nearly the whole runtime in both groups, so a title is baseline, not an advantage. What separates the groups is what the title says: outlier titles state a premise a stranger understands; flop titles are labels, names or in-jokes. Usually one static line or two, often ending in one emoji (😭, 🥺💔, 🙏).

What happened, with a person and a verb:
- "Marlon broke the door handle"
- "Lacy got the Pirate Token"
- "Marlon helps an elderly woman with dementia find her friend"
- "Bro had the entire campus challenging him"
- "Lacy gets kicked out of the Bills & Rams game.."
- "Billionaire got scamed for a hamburger" (sic)
- "TJR Got RUGGED For $45k The Second He Entered"

A line from the clip, in double quotes (the setup line, not the punchline):
- "What were you doing before streaming?"
- "What a beautiful dog.."
- "It's soundproof"
- "Did they cut you?"

A question or story fragment ending in an ellipsis:
- "Where does Los go at night?"
- "A raid changed his life..."
- "First night without Los..."

A short label:
- "The animal whisperer."
- "Bro fell in love"
- "Generational switch up"

Flop titles for contrast: "Shoutout Silky and Adapt", "Aura.", "Bepsy is back...", "Define Betrayal.", "Doing the impossible while streaming it..", "Lacy will be live tonight in Japan at 10pm PST".

Conventions: double quotes mean the words are spoken in the clip; a trailing ".." or "..." signals a teaser. The title states the setup, not the outcome ("It's soundproof", not "He screamed and we ignored him"). A few name the cause ("Marlon broke the door handle") and still work because watching it happen is the point.

**Subtitles.** IRL outliers change on-screen text 2.6 times per 10 s; flops 8.2. Lacy A notes no word-by-word subtitles in 7 of 8 outliers; TJR A finds static titles the common outlier treatment. Word-by-word subtitles appear in a few outliers (Marlon's key line only, TJR's skits and the burger clip) and in several flops. Default: no full subtitles. Use them only to make a key spoken line readable.

## 6. Edit treatment

- **Cuts.** IRL outliers have 3.8 scene cuts per 10 s, median shot about 1 s (Marlon 5.1 per 10 s). Lacy's raw IRL clips are near single takes (0.5 per 10 s). Many cuts are jump cuts within one handheld take. Tighten dead air; do not chop action.
- **Zooms.** The detector counts 2.7 zoom events per 10 s for IRL outliers and 3.4 for flops, which includes camera shake. Punch-ins do not separate the groups. Use them only to hide jump cuts.
- **Layout.** Among IRL outliers, 18 are square 1080x1080, 12 vertical, 3 other. Marlon and SR post square with a lower-third title; Lacy's street clips and TJR's IRL are raw full vertical, often with no title. Both shapes appear among outliers and flops.
- **Music.** The classifier hears music in about 70% of outlier runtime and 55% of IRL flop runtime. Cuts are not on the beat (on-beat share at chance). Music is common to both groups. Two of the clearest IRL outliers have none (the sauna screams, the gas pump), and speech-led jokes keep the original audio on top.
- **Sound.** About -14 LUFS everywhere.
- **Look.** Working IRL footage is often shaky, dark or low resolution. Polish is not the variable.

## 7. What does not matter (shared by outliers and flops)

Loudness; text present from 0.0 s; a static title with an emoji; title position (lower third, median about 0.64 of frame height); duration; music bed; frame shape; face on screen (85% vs 87% of runtime); punch-in rate; beat-synced cutting (absent in both).

## 8. What does not transfer to a smaller creator's raw vlog

- **Celebrity crossovers and fan crowds.** These draw because the people are famous. The equivalents: strangers, staff, security, a friend's honest reaction, a child, an animal.
- **Names in titles.** "Marlon broke the door handle" works because people know Marlon. For an unknown creator, use roles: "My friend", "This guy", "A stranger", "Security", "I".
- **Recurring-cast in-jokes** (Los, Bepsy, Nathan). Even big creators' in-jokes flopped.
- **Stream overlays, chat pastes, meme and anime inserts, AI skits, ad end cards.** None of these exist in raw footage, and the pipeline does not add them.
- **Wealth flex** (supercars, cash, a $3.5M car). It worked for TJR because it was shown. Do not imitate it.
- **Trending audio.** The pipeline cannot add it; see the music note in `pipeline/style.json`.
- **Audience size.** Outlier status is relative to each creator's own baseline; these view counts will not transfer.

## 9. Confidence and gaps

- **Strong** (consistent across all 8 reviewers and the measurements): event over talk; no in-jokes; cold open; premise-stating titles; hard cut after the reaction; outliers talk less and change text less.
- **Moderate:** payoff position (wide spread, 30-90%); 15-35 s length; pause tightening (cut-rate data is a median across mixed footage).
- **Thin:** only about 9 IRL-format flops, so most "flops do X" claims rest on 3 to 9 reels. Format tags are unreliable. Reviewers saw hook frames for only a few reels and worked mostly from packets. Transcripts are Whisper and contain errors. Laughter and shout flags are unreliable under music. There is no data on posting time, hashtags or which audio was used. Moment-type counts are a synthesis from notes, not a coded dataset.
- **Unknown:** whether square or vertical framing performs better for this creator; whether subtitles help sound-off viewers of raw dialogue clips. Test both through variations and compare.
- **Playback speed (added 2026-11-16):** an audio check found 2 of the 25 outliers it could judge sped up (about 1.4x and 1.85x, both Marlon), none of the 8 flops it could judge, and no meaningful difference in speaking rate between the two groups, but 55 of 88 reels had too little clean speech to judge and video speed was not measured. Treat a sped-up cut as an untested variation, not a rule. See `evidence/speed_check.md`.
