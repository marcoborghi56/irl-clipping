## Patterns in this batch
- Short, self-contained comedic or story beat beats advice or opinion: 5 of 8 outliers (out09, 12, 14, 15, plus the pure reaction out10) have a scene with an opening, turn and punchline; 3 of 3 flops (04, 05, 06) are advice, opinion or promo.
- Hook is already mid-action or mid-line at 0.0s: 8 of 8 outliers have words or a reaction in the first second (a question, bounty, shout, "Get out of my son's room!", wide-eyed face); flop04 opens on a host question and flop05 on a quiet face.
- Reaction to a reveal inside a title that tells you the subject: out10, out13, out12 (3 of 3 non-skit IRL/stream) use a static 2-3 line caption that names the event ("TJR SEES DRAKES WATCH COLLECTION and REALIZES...", "Billionaire got scamed for a hamburger"). Flops lack a named-event title (flop04, 05).
- Money/wealth is the topic in nearly every outlier, but it works when it is shown (cash, crowd, watches, cars) rather than argued: 7 of 8 outliers show it, flop05 and flop04 only describe it.
- Length: outliers are 13-47s apart from out13 (72s) and out16 (58s); both of those are one-take, high-speech, escalating. The longest flop (06, 66s) buries the payoff at 75%+.
- Payoff placement: the line or spike lands mid-late and the clip ends quickly after (out09 58%, out10 82%, out14 71%, out15 66%, out12 55%). Flops either have no spike (04, 05) or it is very late (06).
- Edit treatment: word-by-word centered caps with punch-in zooms and music on skits (out09, 14, 15); raw reactions (out10, 13, 16) are barely edited with only a static title. Caption style alone does not separate outliers from flops (flops also use it), so moment choice matters more than polish.
- For raw IRL clipping, out12 is the single most transferable: cold-open spoken offer, crowd, cash, named conflict, static headline, black-bar vertical frame, with only light zoom.
