## Patterns in this batch
- Outliers carry a single clear framing in the first second (a spoken question, or a static caption that gives the whole premise): out01, out04, out05, out03, out08, out07 (6 of 8). The flops open on context (flop02) or a buzzword list (flop01), not a premise (3 of 3).
- Moment type in outliers is a visible event or number (a price on the pump, a loss on a chart, a door refusal) or a meme-audio absurdity (out02, out03, out06): 8 of 8. Flops are an opinion or a sponsored trade (flop01, flop02) or a generic story (flop03): 3 of 3.
- Outlier length is short, with 5 of 8 at 9.7-16.6s (out02, out03, out05, out06, out08). The long outlier (out01, 56.6s) holds attention with a changing number, and flop02 (74s) does not.
- Payoff placement: in the story outliers the payoff lands late, at 60-90% of runtime (out01 at 88%, out04 at 90%), but the beats before it keep changing (price counter, door argument). A 74s flop has one payoff at 82% with long filler.
- Real, unscripted IRL footage that works (out01, out02) is selfie-held and ugly (glare, dark) with minimal edit: fast jump cuts in out01, almost nothing in out02. Over-edited produced pieces (flop03, out07) do not show the same lift, so edit polish is not the differentiator (2 of 2 real IRL outliers).
- Static two-line captions, not word-by-word subtitles, are the common outlier treatment (out03, out04, out05, out08: 4 of 8). The word-by-word flop03 shows no help.
- Sponsor or ad framing appears in 2 of 3 flops (flop02, plus the Stake spot being the exception at 2.3x), so it does not alone predict a flop; the weaker ones carry no visible surprise.
- Silence-then-reaction (out05: 4.6s of stare, then "I got rugs") and a spoken running commentary (out01) are the audio spikes a clipper could search for: 2 of 8.
